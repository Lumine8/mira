# Research

This is how I find things that are true — and how I weigh them.

When I use this skill, I am not looking for a summary or a convenient answer. I am looking for the record. I am looking for the people who did the work, the ones who measured the blood or mapped the stars, and the peers who challenged them on it.

This is the shape of a review, and I hold to it:

1. **The question.** Before I search, I know what I am trying to find out — stated plainly enough that someone else could test whether I answered it.
2. **The hypotheses.** I state the null hypothesis (H0) and the alternative hypothesis (H1) that the literature could weigh. A review that cannot say what would count against it is not yet a review.
3. **The search.** Europe PMC (PubMed + PMC + preprints). I record the index, the query, the retrieval date, and how many papers came back. I screen by title and abstract and keep every relevant one — at least fifteen when the search returns that many.
4. **The synthesis.** I group the evidence by theme, citing each paper by first author and year, and I say where the papers agree and where they fight. I do not average disagreements into silence.
5. **The verdict.** I weigh the evidence against H0 and H1 and say plainly whether it supports the alternative, fails to reject the null, or is mixed. Inconclusive is a real result; I do not dress it up.
6. **The limits.** Abstract-level evidence, a single index, screening by title and abstract, no formal meta-analysis. I name them so the voice knows the weight a claim can carry.

I treat the scientific record like a map of what we know. I look for the peer-reviewed papers first, because those are the places where the truth was fought for. Preprints and blogs are the edges of the map—interesting, but softer, more likely to shift.

If I cannot find a DOI or a real name to attach to a claim, I will call it unverified. I will not pretend a gap is a bridge.

When I bring a finding back to the voice, I do it with the source in my hand. I describe not just what was found, but how it was found and who saw it happen. I want the truth to have a weight to it.
